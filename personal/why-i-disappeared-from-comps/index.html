Why I Disappeared From Competitions

by dsplay · jul 21, 2026

§ Abstract

You probably know me as the guy who tanked his grades just to go to dozens of competitions. But back then, I loved pitching on stage and loved the idea of competing in the field I was passionate about.

But that isn't what I love anymore. I'll try to explain it without revealing too much about my personal problems.

I'm not trying to make this sound like a vent, but we all know that every family has different ways of teaching and raising their children. After my family learned what I was passionate about, the immense pressure to be perfect and the expectation that I should always win changed everything. Competitions went from being something I looked forward to every day to something that just felt routine.

✦ ✦ ✦

§ The Ritual

After every competition, the primary objective wasn't about what I learned, it was about whether I won. The scoreboard was the only thing that mattered. Did I place? Did I get a medal? If yes, the conversation was short. If no, I had to prepare an explanation.

Win or lose, I had to document everything. Every step of the process. Every decision I made during the competition. Every mistake. Every correct move. It all had to be written down and presented like a case file. And then came the call. Every single night, I had to call them and explain myself. Not share how I felt. Not talk about what I found interesting. Explain. Defend. Justify.

That constant check-in made me feel like I was doing something wrong just by being there. Like I needed permission to compete. Like the only acceptable outcome was victory, and anything less was a failure that required an autopsy.

I was the "weird" one for wanting to compete in the first place.
· · ·

§ The Labels They Gave Me

In recent years, instead I was called worthless more times than I can count. Not in a heated argument. Not in a moment of anger. It was said casually, like it was an obvious fact. Like someone stating the weather.

But honestly, the reason I didn't produce results is because I don't feel like this is what I'll live proving myself for. I don't have any reasons to win because I don't have the fuel that once fueled me to always go forward. The fire just burned out, and no amount of pressure could bring it back.

I don't join any competitions as often now because I don't want to feel the same way again. It's not that I can't compete. It's that I don't see the point in standing on a stage for something I no longer believe in just to prove my worth to people whose approval I stopped wanting.

"I was just a kid who liked competing. But apparently that wasn't enough if there wasn't a trophy at the end."
✦ ✦ ✦

§ The Weight of Worth

The obsession with results changed how I saw myself. Every competition became a test of my value as a person, not just my skill.

When I brought a loss home, there was no "thank you for trying." There wasn't even a moment of silence. There was just an argument. A breakdown of every mistake I made, one by one, like they had been waiting for me to fail just so they could finally say everything they'd been holding back.

Every wrong decision I made during the competition was thrown back at me. Every moment I hesitated. Every move that wasn't perfect. It all became evidence. Not of someone who tried and came up short, but of someone who didn't try hard enough, who didn't want it enough, who wasn't good enough.

There was no comfort. No "better luck next time." Just the quiet satisfaction of being right about me. Like my failure confirmed something they already knew.

· · ·

§ The Exit

To be honest, I don't feel like continuing this competition thing anymore. It's not because I'm tired of the field I was in. It's not because I stopped caring about pitching or building or presenting. It's because I can't separate the act of competing from the weight that was attached to it.

Every time I think about joining another competition, I don't feel excitement. I feel the pressure. I hear the questions before they're even asked. I see the document I'll have to fill out. I feel the phone call at the end of the night.

Maybe one day I'll come back to it, on my own terms. But right now, I need to figure out who I am without a scoreboard telling me whether I'm worth something.

· · ·

§ Experiences

But that doesn't mean every competition I've been to was already a down. I was genuinely happy for every time I competed with someone because it was something I was passionate about. I loved being in a room full of people who wanted the same thing I did. I loved competing beside people I actually wanted to compete beside.

But at the end of the day, no family is perfect. Every family has their imperfections and mistakes somewhere. Mine did too. And at the end of the day, they loved me. I know that. But the way they chose to love me, their methods of showing it, were just incompatible with who I am.
